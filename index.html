Jingyi Qiu

Ph.D. Candidate, School of Information, University of Michigan On the 2026–27 academic and industry job markets. jaqiu [at] umich [dot] edu

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Information Science at the University of Michigan School of Information, advised by Yan Chen, Qiaozhu Mei, and Alain Cohn. I study how to turn swarms of AI agents into effective organizations, and how to keep human and AI judges from rewarding hype over substance. I am currently a Frontier Agents research intern at Together AI, where I study how different ways of organizing agent swarms affect how effectively LLM agents work together, including how agents evaluate one another’s work and how they perform in strategic settings. Previously, I was a research intern at Microsoft Research (Economics and Computation).

My other work examines how social media promotion influences job market outcomes through a field experiment (PNAS 2026), and develops LLM-based measurement methods, such as a counterfactual framework for measuring rhetorical style (ICLR 2026). My research combines field and lab experiments, causal inference, and LLM-based measurement, so that claims about what AI systems do can be tested rather than assumed. I received a B.A. from Duke University and a B.Econ. from Duke Kunshan University.

Research Highlights

PNAS 2026
Social Media Promotion Improves Job Market Outcomes
with Yan Chen, Alain Cohn, and Alvin E. Roth

A preregistered field experiment in the economics job market shows that promoting candidates' job market papers on social media to the right audience improves their real labor market outcomes.

Covered by the Financial Times, Michigan News, and New Things Under the Sun.

ICLR 2026
Counterfactual LLM-based Framework for Measuring Rhetorical Style
with Hong Chen and Zongyi Li

Turns an LLM from an unreliable rater into a controlled measurement instrument. Across 8,485 ICLR submissions and 250,000+ counterfactual writings, promotional framing predicts citations and media attention beyond peer-review scores, and rises sharply after 2023 with LLM writing assistance.

Working paper
Perceived AI Use and Hiring Penalties
with Qingyi Wang

In a hiring experiment for a real position, applicants suspected of using AI receive up to 40.6% lower evaluations whether or not they used it; recruiters detect AI use barely better than chance and infer lower ability, not lower effort.

Ongoing · Together AI
Organizing Agent Swarms
Frontier Agents team

How the organization of LLM agent collectives, model capability, reasoning, the agent harness, and the allocation of compute across agents shape multi-agent performance, including agents evaluating one another and agents in dynamic zero-sum games.
